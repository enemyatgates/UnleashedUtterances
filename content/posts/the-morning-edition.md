## The Morning Edition

There is a reason newspapers looked the way they did. Multi-column layouts, dense typography, ink on newsprint — each decision emerged from constraints: the cost of paper, the speed of presses, the need to fit as much information as possible into a finite space.

The broadsheet was an engineering solution that became an aesthetic.

## Why We Miss It

Something was lost when news moved online. Not the information — the format. The deliberate act of folding a large page, of choosing which section to read first, of encountering a story you did not seek because it happened to share a page with something you did.

The algorithm gives you what it thinks you want. The broadsheet gave you what the editors thought you needed, alongside what you wanted, alongside what surprised you.

## The Typography of Authority

Serif typefaces. Column rules. Bold headlines at hierarchical sizes. These were not arbitrary choices. They were a visual grammar that communicated: this is considered, this is edited, this is worth your time.

Whether the content deserved that authority is a separate question. The form made a claim. The form still makes a claim.

## Printing This Post

This post is styled to evoke the broadsheet — dense columns, traditional typography, a layout that suggests deliberation. It is, of course, a simulation. The constraints that created the original are gone.

What remains is the aesthetic — and the argument it makes about what reading can feel like when it slows down.
