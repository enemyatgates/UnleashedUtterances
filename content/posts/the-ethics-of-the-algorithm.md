## The Ethics of the Algorithm

An algorithm is a set of instructions. Instructions written by people, for purposes decided by people, optimising for metrics chosen by people. At every step, a human made a choice.

The claim that algorithms are objective is not a description of their nature. It is a rhetorical move to obscure their authorship.

## The Metric Is the Message

Every algorithm optimises for something. Engagement. Revenue. Clicks. Conversions. The choice of metric is the most consequential design decision, and it is almost never made by engineers.

When a recommendation algorithm maximises watch time, it is not neutral about what people should watch. It is actively indifferent to whether the content is true, healthy, or socially beneficial — and that indifference is itself a moral position.

## Accountability Without Visibility

The opacity of algorithmic systems makes accountability difficult. When a loan application is denied, a resume filtered out, or a social media post suppressed, there is often no human decision to appeal to. The algorithm decided.

But algorithms do not decide. They execute. The decision was made earlier, by people who chose what to optimise and what to ignore.

## The Responsible Path

Ethical algorithm design is not a technical problem. It is a values problem. It requires asking — before a line of code is written — what kind of world this system will help create, and whether that is the world we want.

The answer to that question is not found in the code. It is found in the people willing to ask it.
