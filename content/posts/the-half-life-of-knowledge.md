## The Half-Life of Knowledge

In nuclear physics, the half-life of a substance is the time it takes for half of it to decay. Uranium-238 has a half-life of 4.5 billion years. Francium-223 has a half-life of 22 minutes.

Knowledge has a half-life too. The question is how long.

## Facts That Expired

For most of human history, we believed the sun orbited the earth. For centuries, doctors bled patients to restore humoral balance. As recently as the 1980s, stomach ulcers were attributed to stress, until Barry Marshall drank a petri dish of *H. pylori* and gave himself gastritis to prove otherwise.

These were not fringe beliefs. They were the consensus of their time, held by intelligent, educated people who had excellent reasons for believing them.

## The Rate of Decay

A 2011 study estimated that the half-life of medical knowledge — the time before half of what is known becomes obsolete or incorrect — is approximately 45 years. In some fields, it is much shorter.

This is not a reason for despair. It is a reason for epistemic humility. The things we are most certain about today are the best candidates for tomorrow's corrections.

## What Survives

Some knowledge is more durable than others. Mathematical proofs do not expire. The laws of thermodynamics remain intact. But even in physics, our models are acknowledged to be approximations — useful fictions that work until they encounter something they cannot explain.

The half-life of knowledge is not a flaw in the enterprise of understanding. It is the enterprise of understanding. The decay is the point.
