## The Question Nobody Wants to Answer

For decades we told ourselves there were things machines could never do. Write poetry. Paint. Compose music. Diagnose disease with empathy. One by one, those walls have fallen.

So the question becomes not *what can machines not do* but *what do we choose to keep for ourselves*.

## The Automation Paradox

Every tool we build to extend our capabilities simultaneously reduces the demand for those capabilities. The calculator did not make us better mathematicians — it made mathematics optional.

This is not a lament. It is an observation. The question is whether we are making wisdom optional.

## What Remains

There is one thing no algorithm can replicate: the choice to care about something for no reason other than that it matters to you. Meaning is not computed. It is decided.

The last human algorithm is not a formula. It is a commitment. To pay attention. To remain present. To refuse the comfort of outsourcing your judgement to a system that has never had to live with the consequences.

## The Way Forward

We do not need to resist technology. We need to resist the abdication of responsibility that technology makes tempting. The machine is a mirror. What we see in it depends entirely on what we bring to the looking.
