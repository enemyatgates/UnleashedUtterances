## The Weight of Silence

There is a peculiar heaviness to silence. Not the silence of an empty room, but the silence that follows a conversation that should have happened — the words swallowed, the thoughts left unvoiced.

We have built civilisations on language, yet our most profound moments are wordless. The pause between heartbeats. The breath before a decision. The moment after loss when the world continues turning as though nothing has changed.

## What We Lose in Translation

Every language has words that cannot be translated. The Japanese *mono no aware* — the bittersweet awareness of impermanence. The Portuguese *saudade* — a longing for something you may never have had. These words exist because entire cultures felt something that their existing vocabulary could not hold.

What does it mean that we need new words to describe old feelings? Perhaps it means the feelings were always there, waiting for someone to notice them.

## The Practice of Stillness

Ancient traditions understood what modern neuroscience is only beginning to confirm — that the mind needs silence the way the body needs sleep. Not the absence of thought, but the absence of compulsive thought. The difference between a river and a flood.

To sit with silence is to sit with yourself. For many people, that is the most frightening thing imaginable.

## Conclusion

The weight of silence is not a burden. It is ballast — the thing that keeps us from drifting too far from ourselves. In learning to bear it, we learn to bear everything else.
