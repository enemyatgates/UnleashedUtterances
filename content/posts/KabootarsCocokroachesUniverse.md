## A Note Before You Read

This is not a party document in the way you have seen party documents before.

It does not promise you anything. It does not ask you to trust anyone. It does not have a supreme leader's photograph on the cover. It does not have a helpline number that will not be answered.

It is a founding text — which means it is the beginning of an argument, not the end of one. It will be revised by everyone who joins this formation and finds something wrong with it. That is not a weakness. That is the point.

Read it as you would read a letter from someone who is as tired as you are — and has decided to stop waiting.

## I. Preamble

We are not here because we want power.

We are here because power has been used against us — against the farmer who grew the food and cannot eat it, against the worker whose hands built the city and who sleeps outside it, against the Dalit whose dignity is still negotiated at the caste table, against the young person who studied for a decade to stand in a queue that leads nowhere.

Every party that came before us — left, right, centre, caste-based, region-based — arrived with a promise and left with a pension. They built institutions. We got bureaucracy. They wrote laws. We got surveillance. They held elections. We got the same faces with new flags.

We are done waiting for the right party, the right leader, the right moment.

**We are the moment.**

---

Everyone wants to be a lion, an eagle, a wolf. Symbols of dominance, grandeur, control, power. They put these animals on their flags, their emblems, their logos — and then they use those flags to march over you.

We chose differently.

We chose the **Kabootar** and the **Cockroach**.

Not because we are humble. Because we are honest.

The kabootar is on every rooftop. The cockroach is in every crack. They are everywhere, in abundance, unremarkable — and completely ignored. They need no throne to survive. They need no permission to fly. One holds power in its presence. The other holds freedom in its flight.

**We are K & C. We are everywhere. We are in abundance. We are tired of being ignored.**

---

We begin in Bhiwani, Haryana — Pin Code 127021 — because this is the soil we know. A city that produced champions from conditions of poverty and obscurity. A place where people with nothing but their own discipline walked into rooms stacked against them and won anyway. A place at the intersection of agrarian crisis and worker precarity — which is exactly where our politics lives.

We do not speak for India from day one. We speak from this specific place, this specific condition. We will earn the right to speak wider.

In 2020, farmers sat on the borders of the capital for over a year — without a party, without a command structure, without permission — and they won. That was not a miracle. That was what people do when they trust themselves more than they trust the powerful.

We want to give that instinct a political form.

**We are Kabootars and Cockroaches of the Universe.**
**We are here to be counted.**

## II. The Two Symbols

We do not have a lotus. We do not have a hand. We do not have a broom.

We have a kabootar and a cockroach — and we will tell you exactly why, because unlike every other party, we believe you deserve an explanation, not just an image.

### The Kabootar

The kabootar is everywhere. Every rooftop in Bhiwani. Every railway station in India. Every city, every village, every border.

It is called a pest. It is shooed away, poisoned, netted off buildings. It returns anyway.

The kabootar is historically a messenger — it carried word across walls, borders, and checkpoints when no official channel would. In Sufi and folk tradition it is the symbol of the soul in flight, of the message dispatched into uncertainty with faith that it will arrive.

For us, the kabootar stands for:

- **Power in presence** — it is there whether you want it or not
- **Freedom in flight** — it goes where it chooses, not where it is permitted
- **Solidarity** — it carries the word from one person to another, across every boundary
- **Communication as resistance** — the message that cannot be stopped

The kabootar does not ask for a platform. It makes the sky its platform.

### The Cockroach

The cockroach has survived everything. Every extermination campaign. Every poison. Every boot. Every bulldozer of history.

It is called a pest. It is sprayed, stomped, fumigated. It returns anyway.

It needs no king, no hive mind, no central authority. It survives through decentralised, autonomous adaptation — which is anarchist philosophy embodied in nature. We did not choose the cockroach as a provocation. We chose it because it is the most honest political symbol available.

For us, the cockroach stands for:

- **Defiance** — it will not disappear because you want it to
- **Ungovernable endurance** — it answers to no authority
- **Decentralised survival** — no single point of failure, no single leader to eliminate
- **The refusal to be exterminated** — not as aggression, but as sheer, immovable fact

The cockroach does not attack. It outlasts.

### Together

| | Kabootar | Cockroach |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain** | Sky, movement, communication | Ground, margins, endurance |
| **Action** | Travels, carries, connects | Stays, survives, refuses |
| **Voice** | Message, solidarity | Defiance, presence |
| **Power** | Freedom in flight | Power in presence |
| **Condition** | Ignored despite being everywhere | Ignored despite being everywhere |

They are the same condition — expressed in two directions. One spreads the word. One holds the ground.

Together they are a complete political organism.

> *We survive like cockroaches.*
> *We carry the word like kabootars.*
> *We answer to no one.*

## III. The Honest Contradiction

We must say this plainly, because our opponents will say it dishonestly.

**We are a libertarian anarchist formation contesting elections. That sounds like a contradiction. It is not.**

Every serious political tradition has had to answer this question in its own way. We answer it in ours:

- We do not enter Parliament to govern
- We enter Parliament to make governing uncomfortable
- We do not seek state power to use it
- We seek to stand inside the state's own house and demonstrate, every single day, that the house should be given back to the people who built it

### What We Are Not Doing

- We are not pretending elections will change everything
- We are not building a party that will become what every party becomes
- We are not asking you to hand your sovereignty to us for five years and wait

### What We Are Doing

- Using the electoral platform as one instrument among many — simultaneously with building parallel structures of self-governance
- Contesting seats to deny majority to dominant formations and use that as leverage for decentralisation
- Making visible, through every campaign, what self-organisation already looks like — so people recognise what they already know how to do

### The Three Tracks — Simultaneously

1. **Electoral presence** — contest, disrupt, make visible
2. **Parallel institutions** — build structures of self-governance that work without the state
3. **Ideological work** — make the ideas of the powerful obsolete by demonstrating better ones

We do not do one then the other. We do all three at once. Because that is what the kabootar and the cockroach do — they do not wait for permission to exist.

> *Every candidate we field is a message, not a ruler.*
> *Every vote we receive is a mandate for self-organisation, not for representation.*
> *We refuse to be your leaders.*
> *We insist on being your instruments.*

## IV. What We Believe

### On the State

The State is not our enemy. It is not our friend.

The State is largely irrelevant to the lives of most people most of the time — except when it sends a bulldozer, a tax notice, a policeman, or a form in triplicate that must be filled in a language you were never taught.

People organise themselves. Farmers know when to plant. Workers know how to negotiate. Communities know how to share water. They have done this for centuries without permission. The State did not teach them. The State mostly got in the way.

Our goal is not to capture the State. Our goal is to make the State increasingly unnecessary — by strengthening what people already do without it.

The **foundational enemy is not the State. The foundational enemy is inequality.** The State is the instrument. Inequality is the condition. Even if the State disappeared tomorrow, inequality would find another instrument. We fight both — simultaneously.

### On Power

> *Power that cannot be recalled is tyranny with a time limit.*

Every person who speaks for this formation speaks under one condition: the moment the people who sent them say stop, they stop. No arguments. No appeals. No legacy projects.

We do not build permanent leaders. We build permanent capacity.

### On Sovereignty

We own our own sovereignty.

Not the State. Not a party. Not a leader. Not a khap. Not a corporation. Not a religion. Not a caste council.

Each person — individually and collectively — owns their own sovereignty. It is not delegated upward. It is not surrendered in exchange for protection. It is not conditional on behaviour, caste, gender, or religion.

This is not a philosophical position. It is a practical one. A person who owns their sovereignty does not need to be saved. They need the obstacles to their sovereignty removed.

### On Caste

Caste is India's original hierarchy — and hierarchy is what we exist to dismantle.

There is no libertarian anarchism that ignores Brahminical social order. There is no freedom that stops at the village boundary. There is no self-organisation that excludes Dalits, Adivasis, and OBCs from its own leadership.

Any formation that claims to be anarchist while reproducing caste within itself is not anarchist. It is just another upper-caste club with better vocabulary.

**We will be judged by who sits at our table first — not last.**

### On Gender

Khap panchayats are not tradition. They are patriarchy with a microphone.

The same logic that says the State has no right to control your body says that no panchayat, no family, no man, and no institution has that right either. Women's self-organisation is not a women's issue. It is the foundation of any serious politics of freedom.

Half of Haryana's people have been told their autonomy is a problem.

**We say their autonomy is the point.**

### On Economy

We are not socialist. We are not capitalist.

We are for the person who does the work keeping what they make — and the person who does nothing keeping nothing.

- The farmer should own the land
- The worker should own the tools
- The street vendor should own the street
- The idea belongs to the person who had it

Corporate welfare — subsidies, bailouts, land acquisition for industry at gunpoint — is theft. We name it that.

### On Freedom

Speech is free or it is not free. There is no *but.*

UAPA, sedition laws, arbitrary detention — these are not security measures. They are tools to silence people who make the powerful uncomfortable. We will work to abolish them. All of them.

The internet is infrastructure. Surveillance is occupation. Identity as coercion is a leash, not a service.

### On Violence

Physical violence is for those who have run out of ideas.

We stand for **ideological usurp** — we do not want to defeat our opponents on a battlefield. We want to make their ideas obsolete. We do not want to overthrow the State by force. We want to make it irrelevant by building something that works better without it.

The cockroach does not attack. It outlasts. The kabootar does not fight. It carries the word until the word wins.

### On Alliances

No alliances. Tactical solidarity — case by case, movement by movement, issue by issue — without merger, without compromise of the core, without surrendering the independence of this formation to anyone else's agenda.

We stand with aligned movements. We do not become them. We do not ask them to become us.

- **We refuse to have a High Command** — decisions made by the smallest unit capable of making them
- **We refuse corporate donations** — no money from those whose interests conflict with the people we represent
- **We refuse permanent leadership** — every representative is recallable, at any time, by the community that sent them
- **We refuse to make promises we cannot keep** — and we will keep the ones we make
- **We refuse to ask you to trust us** — trust is earned in action, not in manifestos
- **We refuse to use religion as a political instrument** — not because we disrespect faith, but because we respect it too much to weaponise it

- **We refuse silence on caste, gender, and class** — these are not side issues, they are the main issue
- **We refuse to ask the non-voter to believe in elections** — we ask them to believe in themselves

## VI. The Tagline — Six Words Unpacked

> *Be Present. Be Counted. Be Sovereign.*

### One Verb. Three Nouns. One Tagline.

These are not four separate instructions. They are one instruction in three stages.

### Be Present

The cockroach is present. The kabootar is present. They do not ask permission to exist.

*Be Present* means: show up. In the village meeting, in the ward committee, in the factory floor conversation, in the field, on the street. Presence is the first act of politics. Absence is the first surrender.

It also means: be present to yourself. Know what you know. Trust what you have lived. Your experience is political knowledge.

### Be Counted

> *We are here to be counted.*

This is the central line of the entire formation — and it is a command, not a plea.

*Be Counted* means: stand up and be seen. Register. Organise. Make your number visible. The powerful count on your invisibility. Counting yourself is the first act of defiance.

It also means: make the count honest. Every vote, every signature, every body at every meeting is a count. We count ourselves because no one else will count us fairly.

### Be Sovereign

*Be Sovereign* is the destination.

It means: own your decisions. Own your land. Own your labour. Own your body. Own your vote — do not sell it, do not surrender it, do not give it away in exchange for a promise from someone who has broken every promise before.

Sovereignty is not given. It is claimed. And it is claimed by being present and being counted first.

## VII. How We Work

This formation has no president. No national general secretary. No supreme leader. No high command.

It has a founding circle — a small group of people who wrote this text together in Bhiwani, Haryana, in 2026, and who will now give it away. Once this text is ratified by a broader collective, it no longer belongs to its authors. It belongs to everyone who signs it.

### Organisational Principles

1. **Subsidiarity** — decisions are made by the smallest unit capable of making them. A village decides for a village. A ward decides for a ward. The collective decides only what cannot be decided below.

2. **Recall** — every representative, at every level, is recallable by a simple majority vote of the community that elected them. No process. No delay. No appeal.

3. **Transparency** — all accounts are public. All decisions are recorded and accessible. No closed-door meetings on matters of public consequence.

4. **Rotation** — no person holds the same position for more than one term without a fresh mandate. Leadership is a function, not an identity.

5. **Founder-Inspired, Not Led** — the founding circle writes the text, convenes the first collective, then steps back into equality. The documents carry authority so that persons do not have to.

### On Candidates

Any candidate standing under the KCU symbol must:

- Be selected by the community they propose to represent — not appointed from above
- Sign a written commitment to the principles of this founding text
- Accept unconditional recall
- Publish all decisions, all expenses, all communications related to their public role
- Draw no salary beyond the median income of their constituency

### On Funding

- No individual donation above a published ceiling
- No corporate donations under any circumstances
- No government funding that creates dependency
- All accounts published in full, in plain language, accessible to every member

### On Membership

There is no formal membership process at founding stage. The first act of membership is showing up. The second is signing the founding text. The third is doing the work.

## VIII. To Haryana — And Beyond

### Why Bhiwani

We begin in Bhiwani because this is where we are rooted.

Bhiwani is known as the nursery of Indian boxing — a city that produced champions from conditions of scarcity and obscurity, where people with nothing but discipline and defiance walked into rooms stacked against them. That is not just a sporting legacy. That is a political temperament.

Bhiwani sits in the dryland belt of Haryana — low rainfall, depleting groundwater, agrarian crisis that is not abstract but present in every farm and every family. It has textile mills, small industry, a working class. It has thousands of young people preparing for government jobs that increasingly do not exist.

The farmer's distress and the worker's precarity meet here. The unemployed graduate and the informal labourer share the same street. The city is not a symbol. It is the material condition of the politics we are building.

Pin Code 127021 is not a detail. It is a declaration:

> *We know exactly where we are.*
> *We are not pretending to speak for India from day one.*
> *We speak from this specific place, and we will earn the right to speak wider.*

### The Path Outward

We do not contest everywhere at once. We build in depth before breadth.

The path is:

1. **Bhiwani** — root, organise, demonstrate
2. **Haryana** — expand to districts where the contradictions are sharpest
3. **National** — earn the scale, do not declare it

Haryana is a state of extreme tensions that our ideology can speak to directly:

- Agrarian crisis next to industrial corridor
- Jat political dominance creating resentment among Dalit, OBC, and non-Jat communities
- Farmer movement legacy — the living memory of mass autonomous organisation without party leadership
- Severe youth unemployment — educated, frustrated, and without language for their condition
- The wrestler protests — another spontaneous, sustained, leaderless agitation born from Haryana's own people

Haryana does not need our ideology explained to it. It has practiced it. Our job is to give it language and structure.

### To the Non-Voter

We do not ask you to believe in elections.

We know why you don't vote. We know what you have watched happen every five years. We are not asking you to forget that.

We are asking you to believe in yourself — in your own capacity to organise, to decide, to refuse, to demand. The electoral platform is one instrument. It is not the only one. And if you never vote for us but you organise your street, your ward, your village — you are already doing what we are asking.

### To Aligned Movements

To the farmer unions, the Dalit organisations, the labour federations, the student unions — we say:

We are not here to absorb you. We are not here to lead you. We are not here to put our symbol on your struggle.

We are here in solidarity — which means we stand with you on your terms, and you stand with us on ours, and neither of us surrenders our independence to the other.

That is what solidarity means. Not merger. Not hierarchy. Mutual recognition of a shared condition and a shared direction.
