## The Politics of the Button

A button is not neutral. Its size, colour, position, and label all encode assumptions about who will press it, why, and what they deserve to feel when they do.

The dark pattern — the interface designed to trick rather than serve — is merely the honest face of what all design does at some level: it shapes behaviour by shaping environment.

## What the Default Tells You

The default setting is the designer's confession. When software defaults to sharing your data, it tells you whose interests were prioritised. When a subscription defaults to auto-renew, it tells you what behaviour was desired.

Defaults are not neutral starting points. They are arguments. Arguments that most users will never override because most users never change defaults.

## Attention as Resource

The attention economy did not emerge by accident. It was designed. Every notification, every infinite scroll, every red badge on an icon was engineered by people who understood that attention is the scarcest resource in a world of infinite content.

The interface is the ideology made tactile. You feel it with your thumb before you think it with your brain.

## Designing Otherwise

There are designers who resist. Who ask not *how do we maximise engagement* but *how do we leave the user better than we found them*. Who treat attention as something to be returned, not harvested.

These interfaces are rarer. They are also, without exception, the ones people remember with something approaching gratitude.
