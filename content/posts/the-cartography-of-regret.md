## The Cartography of Regret

Regret is a map. Not of where you have been, but of where you believed you could have gone. Every regret contains within it an implicit alternate self — a version of you who made the other choice and lived the other life.

The problem with regret is not the looking back. The problem is the certainty. We imagine the road not taken with a clarity we never had when we were standing at the fork.

## The Hindsight Illusion

Memory is not a recording. It is a reconstruction. Each time we remember something, we rebuild it slightly differently, influenced by everything we have learned since. This means our regrets are not memories of past choices — they are present judgements dressed in historical clothing.

The alternate self we mourn was never as certain as we now imagine. They were as confused as we were. They just had different information.

## The Utility of Regret

Regret is not useless. As a signal, it is invaluable — it tells us what we value, what we wish we had done differently, what kind of person we are still trying to become.

The error is treating regret as evidence about the past rather than information about the present.

## Making Peace With the Map

The cartography of regret is never finished. New roads appear. Old ones are redrawn. The territory keeps changing.

What we can do is stop using the map to punish ourselves for not knowing the terrain before we walked it. Navigation requires uncertainty. That is not a flaw in the journey. It is the journey.
