## The Invisible Majority

Twenty-seven percent. That is the estimated proportion of the universe composed of dark matter — a substance we cannot see, cannot touch, and cannot directly detect. We know it exists only by its gravitational influence on the things we can see.

The visible universe — every star, galaxy, planet, and particle we have ever observed — constitutes approximately five percent of what exists.

## Evidence Without Observation

Dark matter was not discovered. It was inferred. Galaxies rotate faster at their edges than they should if only visible matter were present. Something else must be there, holding them together, invisible and unmeasured.

This is not unusual in science. We routinely accept the existence of things we cannot directly observe, because their effects are undeniable.

## The Dark Matter of the Mind

The same logic applies inward. The conscious mind — the narrator, the decision-maker, the self we identify as *us* — is a small fraction of our actual cognitive activity. The rest happens below the surface, shaping our preferences, our fears, our instincts.

We know this hidden machinery exists because of its gravitational pull on everything we think and feel.

## Living With the Unknown

The discovery of dark matter did not diminish the universe. It expanded it. In the same way, acknowledging the depths of our own unconscious does not diminish the self. It gives it more room to be curious.

Most of everything is invisible. That is not a problem. That is an invitation.
