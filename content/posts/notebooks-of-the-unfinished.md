## Notebooks of the Unfinished

Leonardo da Vinci left thousands of pages of notes — observations, sketches, calculations, questions. The majority were never developed into finished works. For centuries, scholars treated this as a tragedy. The unfinished notebooks of a scattered genius.

It may have been the opposite. It may have been the work.

## The Notebook as Form

There is a tradition of thinking in notebooks that is distinct from thinking toward a conclusion. The notebook does not demand resolution. It permits the half-formed thought, the abandoned tangent, the question that generates three more questions.

Darwin's notebooks were not drafts of *On the Origin of Species*. They were something else — a space for thinking that had not yet decided what it was thinking about.

## Against Completion

We fetishise completion. The finished product. The published work. The polished surface that hides all evidence of process.

But the unfinished thing has its own integrity. It preserves the moment of uncertainty, the fork in the road, the possibility that things could have gone differently. Completion closes. Incompleteness remains open.

## What We Lose When We Finish

When a thought is finished, it becomes an opinion. When it is unfinished, it remains a question. And questions, unlike opinions, are still capable of being changed.

The notebooks of the unfinished are not failures to complete. They are refusals to close. In that refusal, they stay alive longer than most finished things.
