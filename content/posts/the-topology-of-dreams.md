## The Topology of Dreams

For most of human history, dreams were treated as messages — from gods, from the dead, from the deeper self. Modern neuroscience has replaced that framework with another: dreams are the brain's maintenance cycle, a nightly defragmentation of memory and emotion.

Both frameworks may be more compatible than they appear.

## What the Brain Does at Night

During REM sleep, the hippocampus replays the day's experiences, transferring them from short-term to long-term memory. The prefrontal cortex — responsible for logical restraint — goes partially offline. The emotional centres remain active.

The result is a state where memories are fluid, associations are loose, and the usual constraints on thought are relaxed. It is, structurally, a creative state.

## The Shape of Dream Space

Dream logic is topological rather than Euclidean. Spaces connect in ways that defy physical geometry. A kitchen becomes an airport becomes a childhood bedroom. Identity is unstable — you are yourself and also someone else.

This is not confusion. It is the brain exploring the shape of its own associations, testing connections that waking rationality would dismiss as absurd.

## The Message and the Mechanism

Perhaps the ancient interpreters and the modern neuroscientists are describing the same phenomenon from different angles. The mechanism is memory consolidation. The message — if there is one — is what your brain considers worth consolidating.

What the dreaming mind returns to, again and again, is not random. It is the unresolved, the significant, the things that have not yet found their place in the archive of the self.
