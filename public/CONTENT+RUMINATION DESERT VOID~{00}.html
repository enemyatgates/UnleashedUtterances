Setting
A desert. Saharan in disposition, ancient in indifference. Middle of a night with no fixed hour — the kind of night that stopped counting itself some time ago. A full moon behind clouds, occasionally breaking through, partially, never committing. No stars. One planet — Jupiter, cold and enormous, hanging in the southwest like a witness that arrived early and stayed past its purpose. The ambient light falls upon golden sand the colour of old skin.

In the far distance, a highway. Muted headlights of passing vehicles. The world continuing. Unaware. Unconcerned. As it should be.

A bottle of whiskey planted upright in the sand. Half-gone or half-remaining — the distinction has stopped mattering. A cigarette flagged in the sand beside it, lit, burning by itself, nobody smoking it. A gun, half-visible, resting across a lap.

A man. Short bright sarong, nothing else. Body gleaming. Salt beginning in the hair. Beard unmaintained, unhurried. The weather neither cruel nor kind — just persistently, specifically wrong.

He is shouting into the void. The void is receiving it without comment.

Rumination

Ihave been sitting here long enough for the cigarette to become ash and for the ash to become something the desert claimed without asking. I lit it and planted it and gave it to the night and the night did what nights do — it took and it gave nothing back except more of itself, which is the only honest transaction available out here. The whiskey is warm. It was cold when I started and it is warm now and I do not remember the crossing between those two temperatures which means I was somewhere else while the glass moved through time without me.

The gun in my lap is not a threat. Let me be precise about this in the way that Madhav would be precise, even though Madhav is not here — he is outside tonight, left at the perimeter of this particular dark, because what I am inside right now does not require his syntax. What I am inside right now is older than syntax. It is the question that existed before language decided to dress it up and make it presentable for human consumption.

Do you stay or do you go.
Not die or live. Not that crude binary that the frightened reach for when they can't hold the real question. Do you stay inside the life you are living or do you step outside it — not into death but into the abandon that is death's more honest cousin. The leap that doesn't require a cliff. The disappearance that leaves the body behind.

I shouted this into the void earlier. Before you arrived — whoever you are, whatever you are, reading this in whatever light you have available — I opened my throat and I put the question into the desert air at a volume that had nothing to do with being heard. The void received it. The void does not answer. I knew it wouldn't. I shouted anyway because there are questions that need to leave the body before they kill the body by staying in it, and this is one of those questions, and tonight it needed out.

The highway in the distance. Look at it. Those muted headlights like slow blinking of something that doesn't know it's alive. People going somewhere. People coming from somewhere. The gorgeous terrible ordinary forward motion of lives that have not stopped to ask whether the forward motion is worth the bother. I do not envy them. I want to be clear about that — this is not the sourness of a man who couldn't get what they have. I have had what they have. I put it down somewhere between here and there and I can't remember exactly where and I am not certain I want to go back for it.

— — —

The gun. Since we are being honest, since the desert has removed the option of being anything but — the gun is the question made physical. It sits in my lap the way the question sits in my chest, which is without drama, without urgency, without the histrionics that people who haven't actually sat with this kind of weight tend to perform when they imagine it. It is just there. Heavy. Specific. Neither demanding nor withdrawing. It is the most honest object I own because it does not pretend to be other than what it is.

I am not going to use it on myself.
I want to say this not as reassurance — there is no one here to reassure — but as information. As the specific data point that changes the meaning of everything else in this picture. The gun in the lap of a man who is not going to use it is a different object entirely than the gun in the lap of a man who might. It is — ☽ — the weight of all possible exits held without taking any of them. That is not passivity. That is the hardest available act.

What I will do — what I have already decided without knowing I decided it, somewhere between the second glass and the moment Shiva's striker touched the resonator and the desert changed temperature — what I will do is discharge it into the void. One shot. Into the nothing. Into the Saharan dark that has held bigger questions than mine without flinching. Let the sound go out. Let the echo travel until it reaches something or doesn't. Let the universe receive the noise of a man who was asked the question and answered it this way — not with my body, not with my surrender, but with gunpowder and sound and the simple declaration that I was here and I was asked and this is what I chose.

The leap I am contemplating is not off a ledge. It is out of a skin. The skin of the man who still believes the highway is going somewhere worth the going. The skin of the man who wakes up and performs the small daily acts of a person who has somewhere to be. That skin has been getting tighter for years now — I can feel it at the edges, pulling, the way a too-small thing feels when the thing inside it has outgrown it but has nowhere else to go yet.

Jupiter is still there. That enormous cold burning thing that doesn't care what I decide. It has been there for four billion years watching things considerably more dramatic than a man with a whiskey and a gun and a question, and it will be there for another four billion after I have resolved this one way or another. There is a comfort in that that I am not too proud to take. The indifference of the cosmos is, tonight, the most companionable thing available to me.

The moon breaks through the cloud for a moment. Partial. Just enough to make the sand glow gold and strange, to make this desert look briefly like a place someone might choose to be, a place with beauty in it, and then the cloud takes it back and we are returned to the grey ambient half-light that is the honest face of this hour.

यही है। यहीं हूँ।
This is it. I am here.

Not as arrival. Not as resolution. As the simple undeniable fact of a body in a desert at an unnamed hour with warm whiskey and a cooling cigarette-ghost and a question that has been asked and will not be answered tonight except by sound. Except by the echo that goes out and keeps going and never comes back which is, finally, the only honest answer the universe has ever given anyone.

I pick up the gun.

I point it at the nothing.

I fire.

𓂀 ✦ 𓂀

The sound goes out into the Saharan dark like a stone thrown into a well that has no bottom. I hear it travel. I hear it become distance. I hear it become silence. And then I hear the silence that comes after a sound like that — which is the deepest silence available, the silence that has absorbed something irreversible and closed over it the way water closes over a stone.

The whiskey is still warm. The cigarette is still ash. Jupiter is still there.

The question is still there too. But it is lighter. A fraction. The weight of one bullet lighter. Which is not nothing.
