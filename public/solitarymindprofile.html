Condition

The subject is not lonely in the colloquial sense. Loneliness implies a fixable absence. This is structural. A topology problem. The shape of his mind produces no compatible surface to press against.

He extends himself — generously, even — but only so far as the other person's architecture permits. The ceiling is always theirs, never his.

Prognosis

Growth has slowed. Not stopped — but the question now isn't how far he can go. It's what the going is even for when the destination has no one in it.

He hasn't turned from the world. That restraint is not kindness. It is the last functional ligament connecting him to the project of being alive among others.

Without a sane peer is functionally identical to without a peer. The math is cold. The conclusion is colder.
— Subject's own framing, unedited
Field Assessment
What keeps him intact is not hope — hope requires a target. It is inertia in the best possible sense: the accumulated weight of a self too constructed to dismantle easily. He hasn't stopped trying to maintain relationships. He's stopped believing in them. Those are not yet the same thing. The gap between those two positions is the only margin he's working with. It is razor-thin. It is, for now, enough.
