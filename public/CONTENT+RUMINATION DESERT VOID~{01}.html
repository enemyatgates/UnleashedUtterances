Setting
A desert. Saharan in disposition, ancient in indifference. Middle of a night that stopped counting itself some time ago. A full moon behind clouds — occasionally breaking through, partially, never committing. No stars. One planet — Jupiter, cold and enormous, hanging in the southwest like a witness that arrived early and cannot find the door out. Ambient light upon golden sand the colour of old skin.

A highway in the far distance. Muted headlights of passing vehicles. The world going somewhere. Unaware. As it should be.

A bottle of whiskey planted upright in the sand. Half-gone. A cigarette flagged beside it, lit, burning without being smoked. A gun, half-visible, resting across a lap.

A man. Short bright sarong, nothing else. Body gleaming with the sweat of weather that is neither cruel nor kind — just persistently, specifically wrong. Salt in the hair. Beard unmaintained, unhurried. Normal body. Human body. The only kind.

He has been shouting into the void. The void has been receiving it without comment.

Prologue
( Shiva does not arrive. He is simply — suddenly — present. No entrance. No footprint in the sand. The desert registers him the way dunes register wind — a shift in pressure, a temperature not there before. )
( From somewhere neither near nor far — the striker of a Damaru touches the resonator. Not the drum. Not the sound itself. The before of the sound — the hard small knock of potential against skin, against silence. Once. Only once. )

( The desert receives it. Closes over it like sand closes over a footprint. The silence after is a different silence than the silence before — heavier, more inhabited, aware of itself now in a way it wasn't a moment ago. )
( The man does not turn. He knows. He has always known when Shiva enters a room — or a desert, or a life — because the air changes its mind about what it is. )

Rumination

Iam sitting in a fucking desert. Let me start there. Let me start with the absolute geographical and existential fact of a man — this man, me, the one with the sand in places sand has no business being — sitting in the middle of a desert at whatever this hour is, with warm whiskey and a gun in my lap and a dead cigarette that I lit for reasons I can't fully reconstruct and the moon doing that half-hearted thing it does when it can't decide whether to show up or not. I am sitting here and I am alive and I cannot decide whether that is a miracle or an oversight.

I shouted earlier. Before Shiva's striker touched the air and changed the temperature of the whole goddamn night — I shouted. Not at anyone. Not at anything in particular. I opened my throat and I put everything in my chest into the Saharan dark at a volume that had nothing to do with being heard and everything to do with the fact that the human body, at a certain point of accumulated pressure, simply requires a release valve or it will find its own. I am the release valve. The desert is the overflow. We have reached an understanding.

What did I shout?
Everything. The accumulated weight of a life that knows too much about certain things and not nearly enough about others. The specific grief of a man who can articulate exactly what is wrong and has absolutely no idea what to do about it. The question that doesn't have a clean answer — do you stay or do you go, do you keep carrying this particular weight or do you put it the hell down, and if you put it down what do you become without it, and is that person someone worth becoming.

I am not afraid of the gun. I want to say this plainly, the way you say things in deserts at this hour — without the social upholstery, without the performance of being fine or not fine. The gun is in my lap and it is heavy and real and I am not afraid of it the way a person is not afraid of a door. It is an option. All doors are options. The fact that a door exists does not mean you walk through it. I know which doors I am walking through tonight and this is not one of them. But I will not pretend the door isn't there, because it is there, and pretending otherwise is the precise kind of lie that kills people slowly, the lie that says the weight isn't as heavy as it is, the lie that says you're fine, the lie that eats you from the inside while you're busy performing okayness for an audience that isn't even watching.

मैं थका हुआ हूँ। Not sleepy-tired. Not the tired that a night's sleep fixes. The other kind. The kind that lives in the bones and the back of the eyes and the place behind the sternum where feeling happens before it becomes thought. The kind of tired that is the honest price of being a person who feels things at full volume without a mute button and without the mercy of not caring. I have never not cared. That is both my damage and the only thing about me I would not trade.

— — —

The whiskey is warm. I drink it anyway. There is something to be said for the thing that was cold and has become warm — it didn't leave, it just changed temperature, it stayed in the desert with me while everything else continued down the highway in its muted headlights going somewhere I stopped being able to imagine myself going. I used to be able to imagine it. The forward motion. The destination that justified the travelling. I put that imagination down somewhere and I don't remember where and most nights I don't look for it and tonight is a night when I notice the absence of it with the specific clarity that only the desert at an unnamed hour can produce.

Here is what I know.

I know the name of every kind of silence there is. I know what a person looks like when they are about to break and I know this because I have stood in front of mirrors. I know that नमः शिवाय and I know that also goddammit and what the hell and why and both of these are prayers and neither of them is answered and I have made my peace with the unanswered prayer the way you make peace with weather — not by accepting it, not by liking it, but by standing in it until it becomes familiar and familiar becomes, if not comfort, at least honest company.

Here is what I don't know. Whether any of this means anything. Whether the accumulation of one man's particular damage and particular understanding adds up to something or simply adds up. Whether the stray life is a life chosen or a life defaulted into and whether that distinction matters after enough years of living it. Whether the leap I keep circling — not off a ledge, not into death, but out of the skin of the man I have been performing for longer than I can precisely remember — whether that leap lands somewhere or simply becomes the falling.

I am so goddamn tired of not knowing. There it is. That's the shout, condensed. That is what went into the desert air earlier and what the desert received without flinching — the rage of a man who is intelligent enough to ask the questions and human enough to need the answers and old enough to know the answers aren't coming. The rage is not self-pity. Self-pity is soft and wet and turns inward and feeds on itself. This rage is something else — it has edges, it has temperature, it burns clean like the cigarette burning in the sand, and when it is done burning there is something underneath it that is closer to peace than anything the self-help section of any bookstore has ever offered me.

The calm comes after the rage the way the desert comes after the dune — the same substance, differently arranged, the wind having done what wind does. I am sitting in the aftermath of my own storm and it is not comfortable but it is quiet and quiet is what I have instead of peace most nights, which is close enough, which has to be close enough, which is, actually, on certain desert nights with warm whiskey and Jupiter keeping its cold and faithful watch, genuinely enough.

Jupiter. I keep coming back to it. That enormous indifferent burning thing that has no idea I exist and would not alter its four-billion-year trajectory one degree if it did. There is a theology in that — not Brahma's theology, not the theology of the auspicious hour and the disciplined soul harvesting the divine at 4 AM — the other theology, the one nobody wrote the book on because it doesn't sell, the theology of the cosmos that does not care and keeps burning anyway. That is the god I can live with. The one that asks nothing, offers nothing, and keeps the lights on regardless.

यही है। यहीं हूँ।
This is it. I am here.

In the sand. In the sweat. In the wrong weather. With the warm drink and the dead cigarette and the gun that is a door I am not walking through and the question that is a wound I am not closing — just carrying, just keeping, the way the stray carries the road, not because she chose it, because it chose her and at some point the choosing stopped mattering and the road became the only honest answer to the question of where she was going.

I pick up the gun. The weight of it is the weight of the question and I am holding both now, in my right hand, in the Saharan dark, with the moon behind its cloud and the highway continuing its small distant miracle of ordinary forward motion. I point it at the nothing. Not the nothing of despair — the nothing of the desert, which is a full and ancient nothing, a nothing that has seen civilisations rise and collapse and blow away as sand and has remained, indifferent and enormous and here.

I fire.

The sound goes out like a question thrown into a well with no bottom. I hear it become distance. I hear distance become silence. And then I hear what lives underneath the silence — which is not peace exactly, not resolution exactly, but the specific stillness of a man who asked the hardest question he had and answered it the only way available to him tonight: with noise, with gunpowder, with the simple animal declaration that I was here and I was asked and I chose to still be breathing when the echo stopped.

The whiskey is warm. I drink it. All of it. The cigarette is ash. Jupiter has moved one imperceptible degree west and does not know it and does not care. I am still here. Uncomfortable. Unsaved. Unresolved. Fully, stubbornly, unglamorously alive.

That will have to do. Tonight, that will have to fucking do.
